# Search playbook

Query patterns for each step. Replace the words in angle brackets. Run several queries at once, then open the pages that matter. A query that returns nothing useful is still worth logging when it tests a gap.

## Contents

1. Working habits
2. Market size (Step 2)
3. Business models and players (Steps 3 and 4)
4. Winners (Step 5)

8. Counter-searches for the gap check (Step 9)

## 1. Working habits

- Search in batches of five to ten queries, read the result titles, then open the three to eight pages that carry facts. Titles tell you where to look, not what is true.
- Search in the language of the market you analyse, and use the trade's own words. Insiders rarely use the founder's name for the product.
- Add the current year for anything that changes: funding, prices, headcount, regulation.
- When a page will not open, look for another outlet covering the same event before giving up on the fact.
- After each batch, add rows to `evidence.csv`. Do not leave it for the end.
- Keep a short list of what you looked for and did not find. It goes into section 16.

## 2. Market size (Step 2)

- `<sector> industry snapshot <year> assets clients revenue` (association and regulator reports)
- `<regulator or association> <sector> statistics <year> premiums written` / `fee revenue` / `transaction volume`
- `<sector> revenue pool <country> <year>`
- `how many <customers> in <country> use <service> <year> survey`
- `<sector> expense ratio loss ratio margin <year>` (to split the price into cost, distribution and profit)
- `<largest incumbent> annual report <year> segment revenue`

Read the primary report, or a trade article that quotes it with figures and the reporting period.

## 3. Business models and players (Steps 3 and 4)

- `<sector> startups <year> landscape` / `market map`
- `<job to be done> platform` / `app` / `service` `<country>`
- `<incumbent model> alternative flat fee` / `subscription` / `AI`
- `<company> pricing`, then open the company's pricing page
- `<company> customers` / `assets` / `revenue` `<year>`
- `<company> raises`, `<company> series`, `<company> valuation`
- `<company> form ADV` / `10-K` / `S-1` / the local equivalent filing

For each company, open at least one page of its own (product or pricing) and one independent page (trade press or filing).

## 4. Winners (Step 5)

- `<company> growth <year>` / `tripled` / `doubled`
- `<company> raises prices` / `cuts prices` / `new pricing`
- `<company> distribution` / `partners` / `channel` / `white label`
- `<company> CEO interview <year>` (strategy in their own words)
- `fastest growing <sector> companies <year>`

## 5. Comps, deals and deal lists (Step 6)

Comps and precedents:

- `<company> IPO revenue valuation`
- `<company> acquired by <year> price`
- `<sector> acquisition <year> price revenue multiple`
- `<acquirer type> acquires <sector> company` (strategic buyers, private equity)
- `<company> sold for` / `valuation cut` / `down round`

Deal lists (find at least three kinds):

- Investment-bank sector reports: `<sector> market update <year> pdf <bank>`; boutique banks publish monthly or quarterly sector decks with deal tables.
- Funding trackers: `<sector> funding tracker <year>`, `<sector> deals list <year>`
- Accelerator directories: the accelerator's company directory filtered by industry
- Trade press round-ups: `<sector> funding round-up <month> <year>`

Note how complete each list is and how much of it you read.

## 6. Graveyard and absentees (Step 7)

Graveyard:

- `<product type> shut down` / `shuts` / `winds down` / `discontinued`
- `<company> layoffs <year>`
- `<bank or incumbent> exits <product type>`
- `why <product type> failed` / `post-mortem`
- `<company> acquired after` / `sold for less than`

Absentees:

- `<incumbent> AI <product type> <year>` and the incumbent's earnings-call commentary
- `<incumbent> pilot <product type>`
- `<big technology company> <sector> features <year>`
- `<regulator> rule <activity> AI` / `proposal withdrawn`
- `can <licensed entity> use AI to <regulated act>`

## 7. Demand (Step 8)

- `<sector> survey <year> trust` / `willingness to pay` / `prefer human`
- `<product type> churn` / `retention` / `usage`
- `<product type> reviews` and app-store pages for rating counts
- `<company> waitlist` / `sign-ups` / `users` (and check who is counting)
- `<customers> interested in <product type> survey <year>`

Rank what you find as in Step 8 of the skill: behaviour, founder data, then attitudes.

## 8. Counter-searches for the gap check (Step 9)

For each cell that looks empty, try to find an occupant:

1. **Other words.** Describe the product the way a trade journalist, a customer and an investor would. Search each phrasing.
2. **Funding verbs.** `<segment> startup raises <year>`, `<segment> seed`, `<segment> series A`, `launches <product type> for <segment>`.
3. **Deal lists.** Scan each list from section 5 for the segment. Record "none found" per list.
4. **Accelerators.** Look through the last four to six batches in the relevant industry category.
5. **Neighbours.** `<adjacent winner> expands into <segment>`, `<adjacent winner> plans beyond <its core>`.
6. **Incumbents.** `<incumbent> automates <process>`, `<incumbent> instant <decision>`, industry standards projects for the same process.
7. **Infrastructure.** Platforms that already process the segment's transactions for incumbents.
8. **Graveyard.** `<segment> startup shut down`.

Log every query and its result in section 11 of the report. A gap claim without this log is an opinion.
